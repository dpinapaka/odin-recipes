Pasta

Description

Pasta is a traditional Italian staple food typically made from an unleavened dough of wheat flour (often durum wheat semolina) mixed with water or eggs, formed into distinct shapes, and cooked by boiling or baking. It is celebrated globally for its versatility, acting as a canvas for a vast array of sauces, meats, and vegetables.

Pasta is a foundational staple of global cuisine with a rich history that debunks the popular myth of Marco Polo bringing it to Italy from China, as archaeological evidence shows Italians were enjoying early forms of noodles centuries before his voyage. Originally consumed dry or even prepared as a sweet dessert with sugar and cinnamon, it wasn't until 1790 that the first official recipe paired pasta with tomato sauce, a combination delayed because Europeans initially feared tomatoes were poisonous. Today, there are between 350 and 600 distinct pasta shapes worldwide, all engineered to hold specific types of sauces, and while the plural term spaghetti is globally recognized, a single strand is technically called a spaghetto. Beyond its culinary versatility, pasta offers unique scientific benefits: cooking it al dente (firm to the bite) slows down digestion to stabilize blood sugar, and its complex carbohydrates stimulate the brain's production of serotonin, the neurotransmitter responsible for feelings of happiness. wheat semolina mixed with water or eggs, formed into distinct shapes, and cooked by boiling or baking.

Ingredients

  • 2 Cups of Flour
  • 1/2 Teaspoon of Salt
  • 3 Large Eggs
  • 1 Tablespoon of Olive Oil (or water)

Instructions

  1. Build the well: Pour the flour onto a clean countertop, shape it into a large mound, and scoop out a deep hole in the middle to create a sturdy flour wall.

  2. Whisk the wet ingredients: Crack the eggs directly into the well, then add the olive oil and salt inside the center.

  3. Mix carefully: Use a fork to gently whisk the eggs, slowly pulling small amounts of flour from the inside walls into the liquid until a thick, wet paste forms.

  4. Bring the dough together: Use your hands or a bench scraper to fold the remaining dry flour into the wet mixture until everything combines into a shaggy, cohesive ball.

  5. Add optional ingredients like cheese, or spices as desired.

  6. Knead until smooth: Press, stretch, and fold the dough continuously for 8 to 10 minutes until it transforms into a smooth, elastic ball that springs back lightly when poked.

  7. Let it rest: Wrap the dough tightly in plastic wrap and leave it on the counter at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow the gluten to relax.

  8. Divide the dough: Unwrap the rested dough and cut it into 4 equal portions, keeping the pieces you aren't actively working with covered so they don't dry out.

  9. Roll it thin: Use a rolling pin or a crank-style pasta machine to repeatedly roll one piece at a time, dusting lightly with flour, until the sheet is translucent enough to see your hand through it.

  10. Cut your shapes: Dust the thin sheet with flour, roll it loosely into a cylinder, and use a sharp knife to slice it into your desired ribbon width (such as fettuccine or pappardelle), then unravel the strands immediately.

  11. Boil to perfection: Drop your fresh pasta into a large pot of heavily salted boiling water and cook for just 2 to 3 minutes until it floats to the top and is al dente.
